When we looked at failed deliveries across 120 Pathway fleets, the reason was rarely the driver. Two thirds of them happened at stops where the time window was wrong, too wide to be useful or missing.

Here is what the fleets with the lowest failure rate do.

## The routine

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask for a window, not a day">
    A customer who picked "Tuesday" is out for part of it. Offer two-hour slots at checkout and store them on the order.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import windows with the stops">
    Map your `window_start` and `window_end` columns when you import. Stops without a window get your depot's default, which you set under **Settings → Depots**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Plan with service time">
    A ground-floor parcel takes 2 minutes, a fridge on the fourth floor takes 25. Set service time per stop type so the planner doesn't pack the route too tight.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the ETA the evening before">
    Pathway texts the customer a 30-minute ETA once routes are locked. Customers who know when you're coming are home.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What changed for one fleet

| | Before | After 6 weeks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stops with a window | 41% | 97% |
| Failed deliveries | 6.2% | 2.1% |
| Stops per route | 84 | 81 |

Routes got slightly shorter because the planner stopped promising times it couldn't keep. The fleet still came out ahead, because every failed stop it avoided was a second trip it didn't have to make.

<Tip>
  Under **Reports → Failures**, filter by "No window" to see how many of your failed stops had no window at all.
</Tip>
