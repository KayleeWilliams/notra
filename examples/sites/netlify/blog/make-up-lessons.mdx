Every swim school has a make-up policy, and almost every front desk hates running it. A child is sick on Tuesday, the parent calls on Wednesday, and someone spends ten minutes looking for a free spot in the right level before Saturday.

Starting today, Tidepool handles that loop for you.

## How make-up credits work

When a parent marks a lesson as missed in the app, or the instructor marks the child absent, Tidepool issues a **make-up credit**. The parent sees open spots that match the child's level and age group, and books one in two taps.

<Steps>
  <Step title="The lesson is missed">
    The parent cancels before the cutoff, or the instructor marks the swimmer absent on the deck tablet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tidepool issues a credit">
    The credit is tied to the swimmer's current level, so a Level 2 swimmer is never offered a Level 4 class.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The parent books a spot">
    Only spots under your class size limit show up, and the instructor sees the extra swimmer on their roster right away.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Rules you control

Open **Settings → Make-ups** to set the rules once for the whole school:

| Setting | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cancellation cutoff | How late a parent can cancel and still get a credit | 4 hours |
| Credit expiry | Days until an unused credit expires | 30 days |
| Credits per term | The most credits one swimmer can earn per term | 2 |
| Allowed lessons | Which class types accept make-ups | Group lessons |

<Note>
  Private lessons are off by default. Turning them on lets a parent book a make-up into a group class, but never the other way around.
</Note>

## Numbers from the pilot

Twelve schools ran make-up credits through August and September. Calls about missed lessons dropped by 70%, and 81% of credits were used before they expired. The biggest surprise was the instructors: they liked seeing make-up swimmers on the roster the night before instead of meeting them at the pool edge.
