A cookie banner is a design problem that starts in the tech stack. By the time a designer sees it, the decision is already made. So we moved the decision to the kickoff.

## The kickoff checklist

<Steps>
  <Step title="List every third party">
    Fonts, maps, video, chat widgets, analytics, forms. Write down the domain each one loads from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Self-host what you can">
    Fonts and icons go on the same domain. That alone removes two requests to Google on most sites.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Replace embeds with links or facades">
    A static map image linking to the map, a video poster that loads the player on click.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick cookie-free analytics">
    We use Plausible. Umami works too if the client wants to host it themselves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the network tab on launch day">
    Load the homepage with an empty cache and count the domains. Our target is one, plus the analytics host.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What it costs

Usually less than a banner. A consent tool costs money every month, needs legal review and adds a layout shift on every first visit. Self-hosting fonts takes ten minutes.

## When you still need one

If a client needs ad retargeting or a CRM that tracks visitors by name, they need consent, and we design a banner that is honest about it. That has happened twice in two years.
