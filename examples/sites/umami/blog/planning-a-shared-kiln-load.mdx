A community studio lives and dies by its kiln schedule. Half-empty bisque loads waste power. Overfull glaze loads mean someone's mug waits another week. This is the process we use at our own studio in Porto.

## The process

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set a cutoff">
    Pieces on the ready shelf by Wednesday 18:00 go in the weekend firing. Anything later waits. Post the cutoff where people drop work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log every piece">
    Members tag each piece with their name, clay body and glaze. In Kilnworks, scan the shelf tag and the piece joins the next firing queue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Group by cone">
    Never mix cone 6 and cone 10 work. Kilnworks splits the queue by target cone and shows how many shelves each group needs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Load tall pieces first">
    Plan shelf heights around the tallest work, then fill gaps with tiles and small cups.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Close the loop">
    After unloading, mark the firing done. Members get a message that their work is ready, with a photo of the shelf if you take one.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Firing fees

Charging per shelf is simple, but it punishes small pieces. Charging by volume is fair, but slow to measure. We charge by footprint: length times width of the base, rounded up to the next 5 cm². Kilnworks calculates it from the dimensions on the shelf tag.

<Tip>
  A full shelf in our 7 cu ft kiln holds about 1,600 cm² of footprint. Set that as your shelf capacity and the queue shows load percentage per firing.
</Tip>
