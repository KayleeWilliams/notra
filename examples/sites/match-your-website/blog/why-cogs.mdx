A single full-resolution scene from our constellation is around 800 MB. Most customers look at an area the size of a farm or a building site, which is a few hundred kilobytes of that.

Since API v3, every file we serve is a Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF (COG). It is a normal GeoTIFF with its internal tiles and overviews arranged so that a client can fetch just the parts it needs over HTTP.

## How a read works

1. Your client fetches the first 16 KB of the file. That holds the header and an index of where every tile lives.
2. It works out which 512 × 512 tiles cover your area.
3. It asks for just those byte ranges with `Range` requests.

For a typical field, that is three or four requests and under 1 MB in total.

## Reading one with GDAL

Any tool built on GDAL can read our COGs straight from the signed URL:

```bash
gdal_translate \
  -projwin -122.43 37.78 -122.41 37.77 \
  "/vsicurl/https://cdn.orbitly.dev/scenes/orb_20260818_s04.tif?sig=..." \
  mission-district.tif
```

<Note>
  Overviews are built at 2×, 4×, 8× and 16×. Zoomed-out map views read those instead of full-resolution tiles.
</Note>

## What changed for egress

Since the switch, average egress per API call dropped from 212 MB to 3.8 MB. Downloads of whole scenes still work, they are just rarely needed.
