About two thirds of the planet is covered by cloud at any given moment. Over the tropics during the wet season, a single location can stay hidden for weeks. Yet most of our customers ask for one thing: a clear image of their area, this month.

The answer is a mosaic. We stack every pass over a tile and keep the clearest pixel from each.

## The pipeline

<Steps>
  <Step title="Collect every pass">
    For each 10 × 10 km tile we pull all scenes from the last 30 days. Over the Amazon basin that is usually 25 to 40 passes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mask clouds and shadows">
    A per-pixel classifier marks cloud, haze and cloud shadow. Shadows are the hard part, because they look a lot like wet soil and water.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the best pixel">
    For every pixel we keep the observation with the lowest haze score that is not masked. Ties go to the newer pass.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Balance the seams">
    Neighbouring pixels can come from scenes two weeks apart. We match the color of each source scene to a reference so the edges disappear.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you get

Each mosaic comes with a `source_date` band, so you can see exactly which day each pixel was captured. If you need everything from one day, ask for a scene instead.

<Note>
  Mosaics are rebuilt on the 1st of every month. Tiles that are still more than 5% cloudy after 30 days get the flag `partial` in the response.
</Note>

## Results so far

In September, 94% of tiles between the tropics came out under 1% cloud. A year ago, with single scenes only, that number was 31%.
