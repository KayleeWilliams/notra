Most churn in a level-based game happens in a handful of levels. Until now, finding them meant exporting events and building a pivot table. Level funnels do it in one chart.

## What you see

Each bar is a level. Its height is the share of players who started it. The gap to the next bar is the share who never came back after it. A level that loses more than twice the average is highlighted.

In the beta, one puzzle studio found that level 1-5 lost 22% of its players, against 4% for the levels around it. The fix was a single extra hint. The next week, D7 went up by three points.

## Set one up

<Steps>
  <Step title="Send level events">
    Track `level_start` and `level_complete` with a `level` property. Most studios already do.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Funnels">
    Go to **Funnels → New → Level funnel** and pick the two events.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the order">
    Cadence sorts levels by their number. For worlds like `1-5`, choose **World-level** ordering.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What it doesn't do yet

Funnels only use the level number, not the attempt count. A level that players retry ten times looks the same as one they beat on the first try. Attempt counts are next on our list.
