People ask us the same question in Discord every week: why not just use SQLite? It's a fair question. So we ran the numbers on DriftDB {{ version }}, SQLite 3.50 and DuckDB 1.4, all embedded, all on the same machine.

## The setup

- **Data:** 100 million readings from 2,000 simulated sensors, one reading every 5 minutes for a year. Each row is a timestamp, a sensor id and a `f64` value.
- **Hardware:** AMD Ryzen 9 7950X, 64 GB RAM, Samsung 990 Pro NVMe, Linux 6.10.
- **Settings:** SQLite in WAL mode with a `(sensor_id, ts)` index. DuckDB with default settings. DriftDB with default settings and 64 MB block cache.

Every query ran 10 times after one warm-up run. The table shows the median.

## Results

| Workload | DriftDB 0.9 | SQLite 3.50 | DuckDB 1.4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bulk insert, 100M rows | **41 s** | 6 min 12 s | 58 s |
| Size on disk | **1.3 GB** | 6.8 GB | 1.9 GB |
| Last hour, one sensor | **0.2 ms** | 0.4 ms | 11 ms |
| Last 30 days, one sensor | **3.1 ms** | 48 ms | 19 ms |
| Hourly average, all sensors, 1 day | **38 ms** | 2.9 s | 61 ms |
| Daily max, all sensors, 1 year | 410 ms | 51 s | **290 ms** |

## What the numbers say

DriftDB wins where time-series workloads spend most of their time: appending data and reading recent ranges. Columns are compressed per block with delta-of-delta timestamps and Gorilla-encoded floats, so a year of data fits in 1.3 GB.

DuckDB wins the full-year scan. It's a general analytical engine with a vectorized executor, and for wide scans over everything it's faster than us. We think that's fine. If most of your queries look like the last row, DuckDB is a great choice.

SQLite is not built for this workload, and nobody should read this as a knock on SQLite. Its row storage and B-tree index do exactly what they promise.

<Note>
  The scripts, raw results and the data generator are in the `bench/` folder of the repository. Run `cargo bench --bench tsbs` to reproduce them, and please post your numbers in Discord if your hardware differs.
</Note>

## What we're fixing next

Wide scans are slow because DriftDB decodes every block before it aggregates. In 0.10 we're adding block-level min, max and sum statistics, so a daily max over a year can skip decoding entirely.
