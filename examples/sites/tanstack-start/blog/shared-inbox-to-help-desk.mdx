A shared Gmail inbox is the right first help desk. It's free, everyone knows it, and at 50 emails a month nothing gets lost. Somewhere around 300 to 400 tickets a month, it starts to crack.

## Five signs it's time

1. Two people answer the same customer, with different answers.
2. Someone asks "did anyone reply to this?" in Slack more than once a week.
3. You can't say how long customers wait for a first reply.
4. Bug reports live in email threads that engineering never sees.
5. A customer emails three times and nobody notices it's the same person.

If two of these sound familiar, the inbox is costing you more than a tool would.

## Moving over in an afternoon

<Steps>
  <Step title="Forward support@ to Brightdesk">
    Add a forwarding rule in Google Workspace. Keep the Gmail inbox for a week so nothing falls through while you switch.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import the last 90 days">
    Brightdesk pulls old threads from Gmail with their replies, so customer history is there on day one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create two or three views">
    Start with **Unassigned**, **Mine** and **Waiting on customer**. Add more only when someone asks for them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect Linear or GitHub">
    Turn a ticket into an issue with one click, and the customer gets an update when it ships.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to leave for later

Macros, auto-replies and a help center are tempting on day one. Skip them for two weeks. Write macros for the replies you actually typed three times, not the ones you imagine you'll need.
