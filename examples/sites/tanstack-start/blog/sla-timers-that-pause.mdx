Most small teams promise a response time on their pricing page and then track it in their heads. Starting today, Brightdesk tracks it for you.

## Two timers per ticket

Every new ticket gets two clocks:

- **First response** runs until someone on your team replies. Internal notes don't count.
- **Resolution** runs until the ticket is closed.

Each timer shows in the ticket list as a thin bar that turns amber at 75% and red when the target is missed. Sort by **Due soonest** and your queue becomes a to-do list.

## The clock stops when you're waiting

A resolution timer that keeps running while the customer is on vacation is useless. When you set a ticket to **Waiting on customer**, Brightdesk pauses the resolution timer. It starts again the moment they reply.

<Note>
  Business hours apply to both timers. A ticket that arrives Friday at 6 pm with a 4-hour target is due Monday at noon, not Friday at 10 pm.
</Note>

## Targets per plan

Most teams sell support as part of a plan, so targets follow the customer's plan. Brightdesk reads the plan from your Stripe or Paddle subscription, or from a `plan` field you send with the contact.

| Plan | First response | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free | 2 business days | none |
| Pro | 4 business hours | 3 business days |
| Business | 1 business hour | 1 business day |

These are defaults. Change them under **Settings → SLAs**.

## What beta teams saw

Across 60 teams in the beta, missed first-response targets dropped from 14% to 4% in four weeks. Nobody worked longer hours. The overdue tickets were simply at the top of the list instead of page three.
