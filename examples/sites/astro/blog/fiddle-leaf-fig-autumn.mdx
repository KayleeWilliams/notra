Every autumn our support inbox fills with the same photo: a fiddle-leaf fig with three brown leaves on the floor. The plant is rarely dying. It is reacting to a change, and the fix depends on which one.

## Read the leaves

| What you see | Most likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Lower leaves turn yellow, then fall | Too much water for the light it gets |
| Brown, crispy edges on newer leaves | Dry air from the heating |
| Healthy-looking green leaves drop suddenly | A cold draft or a recent move |
| Brown spots in the middle of the leaf | Roots sitting in water for days |

## Fix it for your window

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="South or west window">
    Light is usually fine here. Check the soil 5 cm down before watering, and keep the plant at least 30 cm away from the glass on cold nights.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="North or east window">
    In October this spot gets less than half the light it had in June. Move the plant as close to the window as you can, and water about a third less than in summer.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Middle of the room">
    Too dark from now until March. Move it to a window, or add a grow light for 10 hours a day.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Warning title="Don't repot now">
  A plant that is already dropping leaves takes repotting badly. Wait until new growth starts in spring.
</Warning>

## What not to worry about

A fig that loses two or three of its lowest leaves in autumn and keeps the top ones is adjusting. If it keeps losing leaves from the top for more than three weeks, run a health check in Fernhill and send us the photos.
