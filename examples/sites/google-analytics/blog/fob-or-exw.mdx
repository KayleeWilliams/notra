Suppliers in China usually quote two ways: EXW (ex works) or FOB (free on board). The EXW price is lower, and it is tempting to take it. Before you do, look at what you take on.

## What changes between the two

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="EXW">
    You pick up the goods at the factory gate. Truck to the port, export customs and loading at the terminal are yours to arrange and pay for. If the export declaration is wrong, the problem is yours too, in a country where you have no office.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="FOB">
    The supplier delivers the goods to the port, clears them for export and loads them on the vessel you booked. Your costs and your risk start once the cargo is on board.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## A real example

One of our customers compared quotes for 6 m³ of ceramic tableware from Jingdezhen:

| | EXW | FOB Shanghai |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier price | 14,200 USD | 14,650 USD |
| Inland truck and export clearance | about 520 USD | included |
| Who fixes export paperwork | You | Supplier |

The EXW quote was 450 USD cheaper on paper and about 70 USD more expensive once the truck and export fees came in.

## When EXW makes sense

EXW works when you already have a forwarder at origin who collects from many suppliers, or when the factory is next to the port. If neither is true, ask for FOB.
